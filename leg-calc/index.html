How the numbers are measured

  • Every distance is from two edges of the seat that meet at a corner: the side edge, and the front or back edge. The mortise is measured on the face you mark it on. The foot is measured from the same two edges, straight below them.
  • For the foot, 0 is straight under the edge and a minus number is out past it. Both at 0 puts the foot under the corner.
  • Height is from the floor up to the seat at the front or back edge the leg is measured from, on the face the mortise is marked on.
  • Seat tilt is optional and is the same for every leg of the chair. It is how many degrees the seat drops toward the back. With a tilt, the page works out how high the seat is at the mortise, and gives the lean of the leg both against the seat and standing on the floor.
  • The resultant is the lean of the leg off square to the seat. The sightline is measured on the seat from the side-to-side line through the mortise: 0° leans straight sideways, 90° leans straight toward the front or back. Both are read against the seat, tilted or not, because that is what the brace sits on. These are the same conventions the chairagram drawings use.
  • Leg length runs along the centre of the leg from the mortise mark to the floor. Leave extra for the tenon and for trimming the foot.
