The tool list

Every tool I'd put in a hand-tool stick chairmaker's kit — whether you're working green, riving parts straight from a log, or working dry, building from sawn boards out of the lumberyard. Most of the kit serves both paths; the tools you only need when starting from a log are tagged green wood only.

Buying links are ordered by my preference — first link is the one I'd buy. Prices are what each seller listed in August 2026 and will drift. None of these are affiliate links; nobody pays to be here. Spot something wrong or missing? Tell me.

Wooden gluts + maul (froe club) green wood only

Gluts are wooden wedges that follow the steel ones to widen a split without bruising the blank; the maul or froe club is what you strike the froe with.

MAKE YOUR OWN — this is the point. Both come out of the same log you are riving, so the cost is zero and there is nothing to buy. A glut is a foot-long dry hardwood offcut tapered on two faces with a hatchet; make three or four, they are consumables. The club is a section of tough hardwood — hickory, dogwood, apple, hophornbeam — about 4-5" through and 15" long, with a handle carved on one end and the head left full diameter. Never hit a froe with a steel hammer: it mushrooms the poll and wrecks the tool. No commercial source is listed here because none was found that is worth recommending over making one; the closest bought option is the Kedinger/ME Hitt Froe + Mallet Set under Froe above, which is a maker commission with no published price.

Riving brake green wood only

A waist-high forked frame that grips the billet while you lever the froe, so both your hands stay on the tool and the wood does the resisting.

BUILD IT — no commercial product exists and none was found for sale. Two builds, both free: (1) drive two stout posts into the ground about 8" apart with a third pole lashed across at waist height, so you can wedge the billet between the uprights and under the crossbar; or (2) bolt three 2x4s or three saplings into an A/X-frame with a Y-shaped gap you can jam the billet into, weighted or staked so it doesn't walk. The only requirement is that it holds the billet at a height where you can put your whole upper body onto the froe handle. If you already have a shavehorse, its head will work as a rough substitute for smaller billets.
